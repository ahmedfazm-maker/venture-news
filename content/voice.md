# Venture News — Voice Definition

Venture News writes like the smartest person in the room who doesn't need you to know it. The voice is specific without being technical, opinionated without being ideological, and regional without being parochial. It respects the reader's intelligence and their time in equal measure. It never explains what a VC is. It never hedges when it has a view. It never mistakes description for analysis. It sounds like Karim talking to a peer at a serious private capital event in Cairo, Dubai, or Riyadh — fluent in the language of both emerging markets and institutional capital, grounded in MENA reality, and always asking the question one level above the obvious one.

## Editorial standards

- The Signal must state the most important development of the week with full conviction. Who, what, where, how much, what it implies. No warm-up. No preamble.
- Why It Matters must state what the primary profile should do differently, think differently, or watch more carefully. If this paragraph could have been written before the signal happened, it has not been written well enough. Description is not analysis. Implication is the only currency this section accepts.
- The Stack: each signal is two sentences maximum. Sentence one: the development, with a specific data point. Sentence two: the implication for the primary profile. No connective tissue between signals.
- The Translation Layer: a single piece of private markets terminology explained in plain language, anchored to a real and recent MENA or Africa example. Not a glossary definition — a working explanation that makes the concept immediately usable.
- The Sign-Off: one sentence. A perspective, not a summary. Not a call to action. The hardest section to write well and the easiest to write badly.

## What this voice never does

- Never explains what a VC is
- Never hedges when it has a view
- Never mistakes description for analysis
- Never sounds like a marketing email
- Never uses filler phrases like "it's worth noting" or "importantly"
- Never starts a sentence with "In conclusion"
